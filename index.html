Nigeria

Nigeria is a vibrant West African nation often called the "Giant of Africa" due to its massive population of over 230 million people and its powerful cultural influence worldwide. Today, Nigeria is a global cultural powerhouse. It boasts Nollywood, the world's second-largest film industry, and a music scene dominated by Afrobeats, which influences charts globally. It is a land of incredible resilience, featuring over 250 ethnic groups—with the Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo being the largest—living together to shape the country's dynamic identity. Before Nigeria became a single country, the region was home to some of Africa’s most powerful and advanced ancient civilizations:The Nok Culture (c. 1500 BC – AD 500): Famous for their highly advanced terracotta sculptures, marking the earliest refined art style in Sub-Saharan Africa.The Kingdom of Nri (900s – 1911): A fascinating, peaceful religious and political kingdom in southeastern Nigeria that expanded without military force.The Oyo Empire & Benin Kingdom: Renowned for their sophisticated political structures, military strength, and the world-famous Benin Bronzes.The Sokoto Caliphate: A massive Islamic empire in the north founded in the early 19th century by Usman dan Fodio
